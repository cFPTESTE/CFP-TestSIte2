The Universe is expanding faster than the standard cosmological model predicts, and nobody yet knows why. This discrepancy, the *Hubble tension*, is one of the big open problems in cosmology, and it is the focus of [Miguel Barroso Varela](/people/miguel-barroso-varela/), a PhD student at CFP supervised by Orfeu Bertolami.

A profile published by U.Porto News describes his path from Imperial College London, where he graduated among the top of his class and worked with Claudia de Rham, to his doctoral research in Porto. Using supernovae and baryon acoustic oscillations as cosmic rulers, he studies modified-gravity models that improve on ΛCDM and suggest that dark energy may evolve in time. His work has appeared in *Physical Review D*, *JCAP* and *Physics of the Dark Universe*.

More recently he has turned to gravitational waves and, with Katherine Freese, to *chain inflation*, looking at how phases of accelerated expansion in the early Universe could leave detectable signatures.
